# Bag Techniques

The bag is your cooking vessel. Getting the seal right determines heat transfer, food safety, and whether your food floats. Master these techniques.

---

## Why Bag Sealing Matters

The bag serves three purposes:

1. **Contained cooking environment** — food cooks in its own juices
2. **Heat transfer medium** — water touches the bag, not the food directly
3. **Barrier against contamination** — sealed against the water bath

Air trapped in the bag acts as insulation. It creates cold spots, causes floating, and prevents even cooking. Removing air consistently is the core skill.

---

## The Water Displacement Method

This technique needs no special equipment. Master it first.

### Step by Step

1. **Select bags** — Use heavy-duty, freezer-grade bags rated for heat. Standard food storage bags may fail at sous vide temperatures.

2. **Prepare the opening** — Seal the bag about 80%, leaving one corner open 1–2 inches.

3. **Lower slowly** — Submerge the bag into your water bath, keeping the open corner above the water line. Water pressure pushes air upward and out.

4. **Seal at the water line** — Just before water reaches the opening, seal the bag completely.

5. **Verify** — Pull the bag out. It should be submerged (heavy proteins sink). Check that no water entered.

### Tips

- Use a container large enough to accommodate the bag with space for circulation
- Practice with water first — the motion becomes intuitive
- For long cooks, double-bag or use a weight to prevent floating

---

## Preventing Floating

Bags float when:

- Air remains trapped inside
- Steam forms during cooking
- Buoyancy from the sealed air pocket

Solutions:

- Add a stainless steel weight inside the bag with your food
- Clip the bag to the container edge with binder clips
- Use the lower rack position in your container

Floating doesn't always mean failure — but it does mean uneven cooking where the bag breaks the surface.

## Vacuum Sealing

A vacuum sealer removes nearly all air before sealing. This is superior to water displacement for:

- Long cooks (over 4–6 hours)
- Extreme temperatures (above 158°F / 70°C)
- Large or irregularly shaped items

The principle is simple: extract air, seal, cook.

### Comparison

| Method | Air Removal | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Water displacement | ~90% | Quick cooks (< 4 hrs), beginners |
| Vacuum sealer | ~99% | Long cooks, best texture |

The difference in results is marginal for short cooks. For extended cooks, vacuum sealing prevents degradation.

## Bag Materials

### Polyethylene (PE)

- Standard freezer bags
- BPA-free options available
- Single-use
- Works for most applications

### Reusable Silicone

- Can be used hundreds of times
- Pinch-seal closure
- Dishwasher and freezer safe
- Requires clipping during long cooks

Both work. Your choice depends on frequency of use and environmental preferences.

---

## Best Practices

1. **Size appropriately** — Leave 1 inch between food and bag seam
2. **Lay flat** — Single layer ensures even cooking
3. **Remove air rigorously** — Mass out air pockets with your hands before displacing
4. **Label your bags** — Marker on the bag tracks what's inside and when it was sealed
5. **Inspect before use** — Check for holes or weak seals
