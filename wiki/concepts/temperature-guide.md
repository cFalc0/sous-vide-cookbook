# Temperature Guide

Temperature is the variable. In sous vide, water temperature becomes food temperature. Master these numbers and you master the method.

---

## The Core Principle

The water bath temperature equals the final internal temperature of your food. No guessing. No gradients. This is precision cooking.

Target your desired doneness and set accordingly. The rest handles itself.

---

## Beef (Steaks and Tender Cuts)

| Doneness | Temperature (°F) | Temperature (°C) | Description |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|-------------|
| Very Rare | 120–128 | 49–53 | Deep red, nearly raw |
| Rare | 120–129 | 49–54 | Red center |
| Medium-Rare | 129–134 | 54–57 | Warm pink center — most popular |
| Medium | 135–144 | 57–62 | Pink center, slight firmness |
| Medium-Well | 145–155 | 63–68 | Mostly brown |
| Well Done | 156+ | 69+ | Fully cooked |

**Key insight:** The FDA considers beef safe at 130°F (54°C) held for 112+ minutes. Medium-rare gives you both safety and the classic texture.

## Beef (Tough Cuts)

| Cut | Temperature (°F) | Temperature (°C) | Cook Time |
|-----|-------------------|------------------|----------|
| Brisket | 155 | 68 | 24–72 hrs |
| Short ribs | 155 | 68 | 48–72 hrs |
| Chuck roast | 150 | 66 | 24–48 hrs |
| Flank steak | 140 | 60 | 12–24 hrs |

Higher temperature breaks down collagen into tender gelatin. This is why tough cuts become fork-tender.

## Pork

| Cut | Temperature (°F) | Temperature (°C) | Doneness |
|-----|-------------------|------------------|---------|
| Tenderloin | 140 | 60 | Medium — moist |
| Chops (1") | 140 | 60 | Medium — juicy |
| Shoulder | 165 | 74 | Pulled — shreddable |
| Ribs | 165 | 74 | Tender-bite |
| Belly | 155 | 68 | Rendered — rich |

**USDA guideline:** Fresh pork reaches safe temperature at 145°F (63°C). Many prefer 140°F for juiciness, with adequate time for pasteurization.

## Poultry (Chicken)

| Cut | Temperature (°F) | Temperature (°C) | Texture |
|-----|-------------------|------------------|---------|
| Breast (juicy) | 140–145 | 60–63 | Soft, silky — never dry |
| Breast (traditional) | 150 | 66 | Firm but moist |
| Breast (firm) | 160 | 71 | Traditional texture |
| Thighs | 148–155 | 64–68 | Rich, tender |
| Thighs (shreddable) | 165 | 74 | Falls off bone |
| Whole bird | 150 | 66 | Cooks evenly |

**Key insight:** 145°F is pasteurized in 2+ hours. Chicken breast at 165°F (traditional) is overcooked by sous vide standards. The lower temperature yields incomparably juicy results.

## Fish

| Type | Temperature (°F) | Temperature (°C) | Result |
|------|-------------------|------------------|---------|
| Sashimi-style | 104–110 | 40–43 | Barely warmed |
| Translucent | 115 | 46 | Buttery |
| Flaky | 122 | 50 | Translucent flake |
| Fully cooked | 132 | 56 | Moist, flaky |
| Firm | 140 | 60 | Traditional texture |

**Salmon:** 115°F for buttery-smooth; 130°F for firm and flaky.  
**White fish (cod, halibut):** 130°F ideal — delicate but safe.

## Eggs

Eggs are the precision showcase. Small temperature differences yield distinct textures:

| Result | Temperature (°C) | Temperature (°F) |
|--------|------------------|-----------------|
| Barely set whites, liquid yolk | 60–62 | 140–144 |
| Jammy yolk (63°C egg) | 63 | 145 |
| Soft-boiled | 64–65 | 147–149 |
| Medium | 65–68 | 150–155 |
| Hard-boiled | 71–74 | 160–165 |

**Note:** Require 45–60 minutes for thermal equilibrium throughout the egg. One degree makes a noticeable difference.

## Understanding Doneness

Temperature is linearly related to doneness:

- **Lower temp** = rarer, more moist
- **Higher temp** = more cooked, firmer texture
- **Time modifies texture** — same temp, longer time = different mouthfeel

Experiment within the recommended ranges to find your preference.

---

## The Safety Floor

Pasteurization occurs at:

- **130°F (54°C)** for 112+ minutes for beef, pork
- **136°F (58°C)** for 63 minutes for chicken
- **140°F (60°C)** for 30 minutes for chicken
- **145°F (63°C)** for 12 minutes for most proteins

Cook below these temperatures, respect the 4-hour rule. Cook above them, extended cooks are safe.
