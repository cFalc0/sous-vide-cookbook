# Searing Techniques

The final step: creating a golden-brown crust via the Maillard reaction. The goal is color and flavor without raising the internal temperature.

---

## Torch Method — Precision

Best for: delicate proteins (chicken breast, fish), finishing pan-seared steaks, odd shapes.

1. Pat protein completely dry
2. Place on wire rack over heat-safe surface
3. Light torch — wait for dark blue flame with hissing sound
4. Hold 2–4 inches from surface
5. Keep moving in circles for even color
6. Rotate thick cuts to brown all sides

**Pro tip:** Torch raises internal temp only 5–10°F vs 15–30°F with pan-searing.

---

## Cast Iron — Classic Flavor

Best for: thick cuts (ribeye, porterhouse), butter-basting.

1. Dry protein thoroughly (surface moisture = steam)
2. Preheat cast iron in oven at 450°F for 20–30 minutes
3. Place on stovetop over highest heat
4. Add high smoke-point oil (avocado oil — 520°F smoke point)
5. When oil shimmers, add protein
6. Don't touch for 30–45 seconds — let crust form
7. Flip, cook another 15–30 seconds
8. Optional: add butter, garlic, herbs for basting
9. Rest 2–5 minutes before serving

**Critical:** Never overcrowd — temperature drops and you steam instead of sear.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Steaming:** Pan not hot enough OR meat not dry enough
- **Gray crust:** Moving protein too soon — let it sit undisturbed
- **Bitter flavor:** Using low smoke-point oil (use avocado, grapeseed)

---

## Quick Reference

| Method | Internal Temp Rise | Best For |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| Torch | 5–10°F | Delicate, thin cuts |
| Cast iron | 15–30°F | Thick, marbled cuts |
| Grill | Varies | Outdoor, large cuts |
