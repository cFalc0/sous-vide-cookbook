# Sous Vide Basics

Cooking food sealed in bags submerged in precisely temperature-controlled water. The result: edge-to-edge perfect doneness that's impossible with traditional methods. This guide teaches the fundamentals you need regardless of what equipment you choose.

---

## What Is Sous Vide?

"Sous vide" is French for "under vacuum." The method involves:

1. Seasoning food and sealing it in a bag
2. Submerging the bag in a water bath at a precise temperature
3. Cooking for your chosen duration
4. Finishing with a sear for texture and flavor

The water temperature becomes the final internal temperature of your food. This precision is the key — you set it, forget it, and it comes out exactly as intended.

---

## Why It Works

Traditional cooking relies on high heat to cook food quickly. The problem: by the time the center is done, the outside is overcooked. Sous vide flips this.

- **Water conducts heat steadily** — no hot spots, no boiling, no charring
- **Temperature stays constant** — food can't overcook past the water temp
- **Time becomes flexible** — you can cook for 1 hour or 72 hours
- **Tough cuts transform** — collagen converts to gelatin over long cooks

---

## The Core Principle

The target temperature in the water bath equals the final internal temperature throughout the food. You're not cooking by time — you're cooking by temperature.

A steak at 130°F (54°C) comes out medium-rare from edge to edge. Leave it at 130°F for 2 hours or 8 hours — the result is identical at the center.

This is the foundation. Everything else builds on this principle.

## Equipment You Need

### Required

- **Immersion circulator** — heats and circulates water at precise temps
- **Container** — pot, Cambro, or insulated cooler
- **Bags** — freezer-grade zip lock or vacuum seal

### Optional Upgrades

- Vacuum sealer for long cooks
- Cast iron or torch for finishing
- Container with lid to reduce evaporation

The minimal setup works. You don't need everything at once.

---

## The Essential Workflow

1. **Fill** your container with enough water to fully submerge the food
2. **Set** your circulator to target temperature — let it preheat
3. **Season** your protein and seal in a bag using the water displacement method
4. **Submerge** the bag — ensure it stays underwater
5. **Set** your timer based on thickness and protein
6. **Remove** when done, pat completely dry
7. **Sear** for crust (30–60 seconds per side)
8. **Rest** 2–5 minutes before serving

Practice this sequence until it's automatic.

---

## Understanding Time

In traditional cooking, time is the enemy — too long and you're dried out. In sous vide, time works differently:

- **Minimum time** — needed for heat to reach the center (typically 30 min to 3 hours by thickness)
- **Maximum time** — when texture starts degrading (varies by protein)
- **Beyond minimum** — texture may improve, especially tough cuts

The sweet spot lives between minimum and maximum. We'll cover timing in detail in [[timing-guide]].

## Food Safety Basics

These rules keep you safe:

- **Temperature danger zone:** 40°F to 130°F (4°C to 54°C)
- **Below 130°F:** never exceed 4 hours total cook + hold time
- **Above 130°F:** safe for extended cooks (pasteurization occurs)
- **Cooling:** ice bath immediately if not serving
- **Reheating:** never reheat and cool again

These aren't optional. They're the foundation of safe sous vide practice.

---

## When to Use This Method

**Excellent for sous vide:**

- Proteins where doneness matters: steak, pork chops, chicken breast
- Tough cuts that benefit from long cooking: short ribs, shoulder, brisket
- Anything where you want exact control: eggs, fish
- Meal prep: cook multiple portions at once

**Less ideal:**

- Quick weeknight meals (preheat time)
- Crisp textures (requires finishing method)
- Vegetables that need direct heat

The method shines when precision matters and time allows.
