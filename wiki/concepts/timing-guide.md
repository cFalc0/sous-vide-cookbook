# Timing Guide

Time works differently in sous vide than traditional cooking. Understanding why helps you use the method effectively. This guide is universal — apply it to any protein, any cut.

---

## The Three Goals of Every Cook

Every sous vide cook serves three purposes:

1. **Doneness** — getting the entire food to target temperature
2. **Pasteurization** — eliminating harmful bacteria
3. **Tenderization** — breaking down collagen into gelatin (tough cuts only)

The emphasis changes by what you're cooking. A ribeye needs #1. Short ribs need all three.

---

## Doneness: Heat Penetration

Heat takes time to reach the center of food. This follows physics:

- **Thickness is the deciding factor** — not weight or shape
- **Thicker food needs more time** — heat must travel further
- **Minimum time** — edge to center at target temp (typically 30 min to 3 hours)
- **Maximum time** — when texture degrade begins (varies by protein)

Rule of thumb:

| Thickness | Minimum Time |
|-----------|-------------|
| 1 inch | 30–60 min |
| 1.5 inches | 1–2 hours |
| 2 inches | 2–3 hours |
| 2.5+ inches | 3+ hours |

---

## Pasteurization: The Food Safety Threshold

Bacteria multiply in the "danger zone" (130°F / 54°C). Cooking above this threshold kills pathogens over time. This is pasteurization.

**The 4-Hour Rule:**
Below 130°F, food must not remain in the danger zone for more than 4 hours total (heat up + cook + hold).

**What this means:**

- Rare steak at 120°F: max 2–4 hours
- Medium-rare at 134°F: safe for 24–72 hours
- Anything above 130°F: safe for extended cooks

**When in doubt:** raise your temperature or reduce cook time. Both keep you safe.

---

## Tenderization: Collagen Conversion

Collagen is the connective tissue in tough cuts — it converts to gelatin at 160°F+ (71°C+). This transformation requires time.

- **Short ribs, brisket, shoulder:** 24–72 hours at 130–150°F
- **After 24 hours:** texture noticeably softer
- **After 48 hours:** fully tender, shreddable
- **Beyond 72 hours:** diminishing returns

This is the sous vide superpower: tough cuts become fork-tender while maintaining a pink center.

## Thickness Adjustments

Time is about geometry, not weight. Adjust:

- **Add 30 minutes** per inch over 1.5"
- **Double-sided proteins:** treat each side's thickness independently
- **Irregular shapes:** use the thickest point for timing

---

## The Interaction of Time and Temperature

Time and temperature trade off:

- **Lower temp + longer time** = same result as higher temp + shorter time
- **Exception:** texture transformation only happens above certain thresholds
- **Safety is non-negotiable:** don't trade time for safe temperatures

---

## Extending Your Cooks

What if you need more time than the maximum?

- **Raise temperature** to enter safe zone
- **Ice bath** and refrigerate, resume later (within same day)
- **Cook in batches** rather than extending
