# Filet Mignon

Filet mignon is the most tender cut of beef, taken from the center of the tenderloin with very little fat or connective tissue. Sous vide cooking ensures this lean steak stays moist and reaches the exact doneness you prefer without the risk of overcooking.

---

## Ingredients

- 1 filet mignon steak (1.5 to 2 inches thick)
- Salt (kosher or sea salt)
- Freshly ground black pepper
- Optional: 1 teaspoon butter per steak
- Optional: fresh thyme sprig

---

## Steps

1. Preheat your sous vide precision cooker to 130°F (54°C) for medium-rare.
2. Season the filet mignon with salt and pepper on all sides.
3. Place the steak in a vacuum-sealable bag with optional butter and herbs.
4. Remove air using a vacuum sealer or the water displacement method.
5. Submerge the sealed bag in the water bath and cook for 1.5 to 2 hours.
6. Remove the bag from the water and extract the steak.
7. Pat the steak thoroughly dry with paper towels.
8. Heat a cast-iron skillet over high heat with a small amount of oil or ghee.
9. Sear the steak for 1 to 2 minutes per side, including the edges, until browned.
10. Let the steak rest for 5 minutes before serving.

---

## Time and Temperature

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Temperature | 130°F / 54°C |
| Time | 1.5 hours |
| Thickness | 1.5 to 2 inches |
| Difficulty | 2/5 |

---

## Why Sous Vide Works Here

Filet mignon is extremely lean with minimal fat to protect it from drying out during traditional high-heat cooking. Sous vide eliminates the guesswork and keeps the steak at a precise temperature, preventing the overcooking that can make this expensive cut tough or dry. The gentle heat preserves the buttery texture that makes filet mignon special.

---

## Tips

- Because filet mignon is so lean, consider using a temperature at the lower end of your preferred doneness range.
- For a 1-inch thick filet, reduce cook time to 45 minutes to 1 hour.
- Do not cook below 130°F for longer than 2.5 hours for food safety.
- The steak can remain in the bath for up to 4 hours total with only slight texture changes.
- Use ghee or a high-smoke-point oil for searing to avoid burning the fat.
