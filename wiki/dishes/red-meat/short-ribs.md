# Short Ribs

Short ribs are a collagen-rich, deeply marbled cut that transforms into an incredibly tender dish after a long, slow sous vide cook. The extended cooking time breaks down tough connective tissue while keeping the meat juicy, resulting in a texture that ranges from steak-like to fall-apart tender.

---

## Ingredients

- 4 beef short ribs (bone-in or boneless, about 2 pounds total)
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper
- Optional: 2 cloves garlic, minced
- Optional: fresh herbs (thyme or rosemary)

---

## Steps

1. Preheat your sous vide precision cooker to 144°F (62°C) for medium doneness and fork-tender texture.
2. Season the short ribs generously with salt and pepper on all sides.
3. Place the ribs in a vacuum-sealable bag with optional garlic and herbs.
4. Remove air using a vacuum sealer or the water displacement method.
5. Submerge the sealed bag in the water bath and cook for 48 hours.
6. Remove the bag from the water and take out the ribs.
7. Pat the ribs dry with paper towels.
8. Heat a cast-iron skillet over high heat with oil, or prepare a hot grill.
9. Sear the ribs for 1 to 2 minutes per side until a deep brown crust forms.
10. Let the ribs rest for 10 minutes before serving.

## Why Sous Vide Works Here

Short ribs contain large amounts of collagen that must be broken down through long, slow cooking to become tender. Sous vide allows you to hold the meat at a precise temperature for 24 to 72 hours without overcooking, converting tough connective tissue into silky gelatin. This method gives you control over the final texture, from sliceable steak-like ribs to shreddable, braise-style meat.

---

## Tips

- Cook time is measured in hours or days because collagen breakdown, not thickness, determines doneness.
- At 135°F for 48 hours, the ribs will have a rosy, steak-like texture that can be sliced.
- At 155°F to 175°F for 24 to 48 hours, the ribs become shreddable and fall-apart tender.
- You can cook from frozen by adding the ribs directly to the bath; the 48-hour cook time accommodates this.
- After the sous vide step, ribs can be refrigerated for up to 4 days before searing and serving.
