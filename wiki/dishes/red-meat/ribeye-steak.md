# Ribeye Steak

Ribeye is a heavily marbled, flavorful cut that becomes exceptionally tender and juicy when cooked sous vide. The precision temperature control ensures the fat renders perfectly while keeping the meat at your exact preferred doneness from edge to edge.

---

## Ingredients

- 1 ribeye steak (1.5 to 2 inches thick)
- Salt (kosher or sea salt)
- Freshly ground black pepper
- Optional: fresh thyme or rosemary sprigs
- Optional: 1 clove garlic, smashed

---

## Steps

1. Preheat your sous vide precision cooker to 130°F (54°C) for medium-rare.
2. Season the ribeye generously on all sides with salt and pepper.
3. Place the steak in a vacuum-sealable bag with optional aromatics like thyme and garlic.
4. Remove air using a vacuum sealer or the water displacement method with a zipper-lock bag.
5. Submerge the sealed bag in the preheated water bath and cook for 2 hours.
6. Remove the bag from the water and take out the steak.
7. Pat the steak completely dry with paper towels (this is critical for a good sear).
8. Heat a cast-iron skillet over high heat with a small amount of high-smoke-point oil.
9. Sear the steak for 1 to 2 minutes per side, including the edges, until a deep brown crust forms.
10. Rest the steak for 5 to 10 minutes before slicing and serving.

---

## Time and Temperature

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Temperature | 130°F / 54°C |
| Time | 2 hours |
| Thickness | 1.5 to 2 inches |
| Difficulty | 2/5 |

---

## Why Sous Vide Works Here

Ribeye contains abundant intramuscular fat that needs gentle, even heat to render properly without overcooking the meat. Sous vide holds the steak at the perfect temperature throughout, allowing the marbling to melt into the meat while maintaining a consistent medium-rare doneness from the center to the edges. This method eliminates the temperature gradient found in traditionally cooked steaks.

---

## Tips

- Pat the steak extremely dry before searing to achieve a proper crust without steaming the meat.
- For a thinner 1-inch steak, reduce the cook time to 1 hour.
- Do not cook below 130°F for longer than 2.5 hours for food safety reasons.
- The sear will raise the internal temperature by approximately 3°F, so factor this into your target temperature.
- You can hold the cooked steak in the water bath for up to 4 hours total without quality loss.
