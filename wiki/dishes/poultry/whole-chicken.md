# Whole Chicken

Sous vide whole chicken delivers uniformly juicy meat throughout the bird with minimal effort, though it requires proper preparation to ensure even cooking and food safety. By spatchcocking the chicken or filling the cavity with aromatics, you can achieve restaurant-quality results that stay moist even when reheated.

---

## Ingredients

- 1 whole chicken (3-4 pounds)
- 2 teaspoons kosher salt
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper (optional)
- 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
- 1 lemon, sliced
- 1/2 onion, sliced
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme or rosemary
- 2 tablespoons butter (for searing)

---

## Steps

1. Preheat water bath to 150°F (66°C) using a sous vide circulator.
2. Spatchcock the chicken by removing the backbone with poultry shears and flattening the bird, OR leave whole and prepare aromatics.
3. Mix salt, paprika, cayenne, and black pepper to create a dry rub. Season chicken thoroughly inside and out.
4. If cooking whole, stuff the cavity with lemon slices, onion slices, and herb sprigs to displace air and aid heat transfer.
5. Place chicken in a large vacuum-seal bag or turkey-size zipper-lock bag. For whole chicken, ensure no air pockets remain in the cavity.
6. Remove air using vacuum sealer or water displacement method. Seal the bag securely.
7. Submerge bag in water bath ensuring chicken is fully underwater. Weigh down with a wet kitchen towel if needed.
8. Cook for 6 hours to ensure full pasteurization throughout the bird.
9. Remove bag from water and carefully take out the chicken. Pat completely dry with paper towels.
10. Heat butter in a cast iron skillet over high heat. Sear chicken on all sides until skin is golden and crispy, about 5 minutes total.
11. Alternatively, place chicken under a preheated broiler for 5-7 minutes until skin crisps.
12. Let rest for 5-10 minutes before carving and serving.

| Parameter | Value |

---

## Why Sous Vide Works Here

Whole chicken normally suffers from uneven cooking where the breast dries out before the thighs are done, but sous vide eliminates this problem by holding the entire bird at a precise temperature. The long cook time at 150°F ensures pasteurization while keeping the meat juicy and tender throughout. Stuffing the cavity with aromatics improves heat transfer and infuses the meat with flavor from the inside out.

---

## Tips

- Spatchcocking (removing backbone and flattening) promotes even cooking and reduces cook time compared to whole bird
- If cooking whole, filling the cavity with onions and lemons removes air pockets that can harbor bacteria
- The 6-hour cook time is necessary for pasteurization at 150°F; do not shorten this time
- Chill the cooked chicken in an ice bath before searing if you want to serve it later; it will keep refrigerated for up to 4 days
- Reserve any juices from the bag to make a gravy or pan sauce
