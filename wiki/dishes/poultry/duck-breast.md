# Duck Breast

Sous vide duck breast produces restaurant-quality results with a uniformly pink, juicy interior and crisp skin that is difficult to achieve with traditional stovetop methods alone. As a meat best served medium-rare, duck breast is an ideal candidate for sous vide cooking, which eliminates the guesswork and prevents the meat from turning gray and tough.

---

## Ingredients

- 2 boneless duck breasts (5-6 ounces each)
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt
- 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme (optional)
- 1 tablespoon vegetable oil (for searing)

---

## Steps

1. Using a sharp knife, score the skin in a crosshatch pattern, cutting through the fat but not into the meat.
2. Pat duck breasts completely dry with paper towels. Season both sides generously with salt and pepper.
3. For better skin rendering, place duck skin-side down in a cold nonstick skillet. Heat over medium-high heat for 6-8 minutes until fat renders and skin is lightly browned.
4. Flip duck, add thyme sprigs to skillet, and cook for 30 seconds. Transfer duck to a plate and discard rendered fat.
5. Preheat water bath to 135°F (57°C) using a sous vide circulator for medium doneness.
6. Place duck breasts and thyme sprigs in a zipper-lock bag. Seal using water displacement method.
7. Submerge bag in water bath ensuring duck is fully underwater. Cook for 1.5 to 2 hours.
8. Remove duck from bag and pat completely dry with paper towels.
9. Heat oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until just smoking. Place duck skin-side down and cook until well-browned and crisp, 2-4 minutes.
10. Transfer to a cutting board and let rest for 5 minutes.
11. Slice crosswise into 1/2-inch strips and serve immediately.

## Why Sous Vide Works Here

Duck breast is traditionally served medium-rare, but pan-searing alone often leads to overcooked meat by the time the thick layer of fat under the skin has rendered and crisped. Sous vide solves this by gently cooking the meat to the exact doneness while the pre-sear step renders much of the fat. The thick skin acts as an insulator, allowing you to crisp it afterward without overcooking the tender pink interior.

---

## Tips

- Score the skin in a crosshatch pattern to help fat render and prevent shrinkage during cooking
- Starting the duck in a cold pan lets the fat render slowly without burning the skin
- For rare duck, cook at 130°F (54°C); for medium, use 135°F (57°C); for well-done, use 150°F (65°C)
- Chill the duck in the refrigerator for 10 minutes after sous vide for easier slicing
- Duck can be cooked from frozen by adding 30 minutes to the cook time
- Reserve rendered duck fat from the pre-sear step for roasting potatoes or other vegetables
