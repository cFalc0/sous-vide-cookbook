# Ahi Tuna Tataki

This sashimi-style preparation leaves ahi tuna buttery and rare at the center, with only the outer edges seared for a smoky, textural contrast. Sous vide cooks the tuna to a precise 110°F, ensuring uniform doneness that traditional searing alone cannot achieve, then a quick torch or pan sear finishes the tataki style.

---

## Ingredients

- 2 ahi tuna steaks (1.5 inches thick, 8 oz each)
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp freshly grated ginger
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp white sesame seeds
- 1 tbsp black sesame seeds
- 1 scallion, thinly sliced (for garnish)
- 1 tsp togarashi spice (for garnish)

---

## Steps

1. Preheat sous vide water bath to 110°F (43°C).
2. In a small bowl, whisk together sesame oil, soy sauce, ginger, and black pepper.
3. Place tuna steaks in a bag, pour marinade over them, and massage to coat evenly.
4. Seal bag using water displacement method, then submerge in water bath.
5. Cook for 30-45 minutes for 1.5-inch steaks.
6. Remove bag from water, take out tuna and pat completely dry with paper towels.
7. Press sesame seeds into all sides of the tuna steaks to coat.
8. Heat a cast-iron skillet over high heat until smoking, or use a kitchen torch.
9. Sear only the outer edges of the tuna for 10-15 seconds per side, leaving the center raw.
10. Slice thinly against the grain, garnish with scallion and togarashi, and serve.

---

## Why Sous Vide Works Here

Ahi tuna transforms from tender to canned-texture in just 5°F, making precise temperature control essential for tataki. Sous vide gently warms the tuna to 110°F for uniform, sashimi-level doneness throughout, so only the outer edges need a quick sear to achieve the signature tataki crust without overcooking the center.

---

## Tips

- Use sushi-grade tuna frozen to FDA standards for safe raw consumption.
- Chill tuna in the fridge for 20 minutes after the bath to firm up before searing.
- Brush tuna with extra sesame oil before searing for a crispier, more flavorful crust.
- Serve with ponzu sauce and pickled ginger for a traditional Japanese accompaniment.
