# Salmon Lemon Dill

This bright, herb-forward take on classic sous vide salmon pairs flaky, buttery fish with sharp citrus and fresh dill. Sous vide locks in moisture at 115°F, delivering a translucent, tender texture that traditional pan-searing often overcooks.

---

## Ingredients

- 4 salmon fillets (6 oz each, 1 inch thick)
- 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
- 1 tbsp lemon zest
- 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 3 tbsp fresh dill, chopped
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 2 garlic cloves, minced

---

## Steps

1. Preheat sous vide water bath to 115°F (46°C).
2. In a small bowl, whisk together olive oil, lemon zest, lemon juice, dill, salt, pepper, and garlic.
3. Place salmon fillets in a single layer in a vacuum-sealable bag or zipper-lock bag.
4. Pour lemon-dill mixture over salmon, ensuring even coating.
5. Remove air using water displacement method or vacuum sealer, then submerge bag in water bath.
6. Cook for 30-45 minutes for 1-inch fillets, or 45-60 minutes for thicker cuts.
7. Remove bag from water, pat salmon dry with paper towels.
8. Optional: Sear skin-side down in a hot skillet for 30 seconds to crisp, if desired.
9. Serve immediately with extra fresh dill and lemon wedges.

---

## Time and Temperature

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Temperature | 115°F / 46°C |

---

## Why Sous Vide Works Here

Salmon is extremely sensitive to temperature fluctuations, with just 5°F separating buttery-tender from dry, chalky textures. Sous vide eliminates guesswork, holding the fish at exactly 115°F to achieve a translucent, flaky consistency that retains all its natural oils and pairs perfectly with bright lemon and dill.

---

## Tips

- Dry-brine salmon with salt 15 minutes before cooking for firmer, more flavorful flesh.
- Add a sprig of fresh rosemary or thyme to the bag for extra aromatic depth.
- Chill cooked salmon after the bath for 10 minutes before searing to prevent flaking.
- Reserve leftover lemon-dill sauce from the bag to drizzle over steamed vegetables or rice.
